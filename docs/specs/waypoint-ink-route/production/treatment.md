# Waypoint Ink Route — Approved Treatment

**Status:** Direction approved on 2026-08-31. Preproduction and tracer implementation remain unapproved.

## Direction

- **Primary mode:** Cinematic narrative with a restrained storybook-chart environment.
- **Provisional dials:** Motion 4, space 4, input 1, immersion 3, density 1. These values describe the selected direction but still need preproduction calibration.
- **Premise:** The visitor does not arrive at a world; after choosing the journey, they watch Waypoint author the world into existence.
- **Audience-facing theme:** A minimalist animated-film map travel sequence created live on Driftwood Paper.
- **Product truth:** Waypoint retains precise visual feedback, gives an agent a trustworthy route through the work, and returns inspectable Resolution evidence.
- **Conflict → change:** A visual observation without an execution path becomes an authored route from Annotation to retained result.
- **Primary feeling:** Setting out with purpose.
- **Irresistible actions:** Get Waypoint immediately or enter the Ink Route to understand how it works.
- **Preproduction order:** Shot plan / storyboard first, followed by a technical scout and bounded tracer proposal.

## Cinematographic thesis

The homepage begins as a useful product hero printed on Driftwood Paper. The visitor can act immediately or deliberately enter the journey. Journey activation is the causal edit: the hero clears, the frame holds on empty paper, and a black ink impact blooms into the first dashed route. The route then behaves as the traveling subject. As native scroll advances it through Annotation, Queue, Agent, and Resolution, sparse map geometry and product evidence appear only when the route gives them meaning.

The frame starts almost empty and becomes legible rather than merely more decorated. Waypoint's semantic colors enter at product beats; paper and ink remain dominant. Sound is not ambience pasted over the sequence: responsive ink-on-paper foley follows the visible route and becomes silent when the route is not being drawn.

## Story spine

1. **Promise — Immediate agency on paper.** The hero identifies Waypoint, keeps the product action and Docs available, and offers the optional journey.
2. **Gate — See the journey.** A tactile activation deliberately leaves the conventional hero and authorizes synchronized sound.
3. **Beat — Empty paper becomes intention.** Hero content exits, a short blank hold creates anticipation, and black ink establishes the route origin.
4. **Beat — Annotation gives the journey a precise starting point.** The first destination turns a visual observation into retained context.
5. **Beat — Queue makes the route durable.** The same identity arrives as local, inspectable work rather than a disposable message.
6. **Beat — Agent turns retained context into action.** The route reaches an MCP-compatible agent and visibly changes from outbound direction to returning work.
7. **Reversal — The route comes back.** What first appeared to be a one-way handoff returns with implementation and evidence.
8. **Payoff — Resolution restores agency.** The loop completes, the visitor can inspect what Waypoint retains, and the product action returns naturally.

## Approved visual and interaction language

- **Canvas:** Driftwood Paper `#e9e1d3`.
- **Typography:** Existing Waypoint Poppins, Literata, and IBM Plex Mono roles.
- **Route:** Black-brown organic ink, initially sparse, becoming a dashed traveling path.
- **Color progression:** Signal Rust belongs to Annotation; Verdigris enters with constructive progress and Queue; Deep Ocean strengthens around Agent; Brass remains fine bearing and completion detail.
- **Journey activation:** Tactile press → hero clear → blank-paper hold → ink impact → first route segment.
- **Journey input:** Native scroll after the activation transition, with pointer, touch, and keyboard-compatible entry.
- **Sound:** User-initiated, mutable, render-synchronized ink contact and paper-fiber drawing. No narration and no sound-dependent meaning.
- **World density:** Minimal route-first composition rather than a populated treasure-map spectacle.

## Reference frames and anti-references

- [Chartroom Wonder concept](../../../references/waypoint-storybook-chart/chartroom-wonder-concept.png) provides composition and emotional evidence, not production copy, typography, or pixel geometry.
- Classic animated adventure-film **travel montages** and **map travel sequences** provide cinematic language: causal route drawing, destination reveals, journey compression, and purposeful sound.
- Anti-references are the discarded existing homepage, a completed map at entry, a forced intro, a dense pirate-map pastiche, and a mascot performance that replaces product proof.

## Coverage constraints

- **Input paths:** Immediate product CTA, journey activation by pointer/touch/keyboard, direct native scrolling, ordinary document navigation, and a persistent mute path.
- **Reduced-motion direction:** Preserve the complete story as a static or discretely revealed chart with no required spatial travel, ink bloom, or continuous scrubbing.
- **Muted/unsupported direction:** Preserve every beat and product action without audio.
- **Mobile direction:** Recompose the travel montage as a deliberate vertical journey; do not shrink or crop the desktop frame.
- **Device floor:** Exact baseline remains open for the technical scout; a current mid-range mobile browser at 375 CSS pixels is the working assumption, not an approved final budget.
